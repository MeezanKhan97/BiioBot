The same starting material can follow different pathways depending on what it is and local conditions.

Note: not every type of biomass should automatically become fuel — dry leaves may be better composted, wet organic waste often suits biogas, and seasoned wood may suit direct or improved combustion. The right pathway depends on the material and local conditions.

Biogas

Produced by anaerobic digestion of cow dung, kitchen waste and other organic matter in a biogas plant, yielding gas for cooking/lighting and digestate as manure.

Biomass pellets

Agricultural residue or sawdust compressed into dense, uniform pellets that burn more efficiently and are easier to store and transport than loose residue.

Biomass briquettes

Similar to pellets but larger and blockier, briquettes are made by compacting crop residue or wood waste for use in furnaces, boilers or improved stoves.

Improved combustion

Improved cookstoves and controlled-combustion devices burn traditional fuels like firewood more completely, cutting smoke and fuel use compared to open fires.

Use Biomass Responsibly

Small changes in how biomass is used make a real difference.

  • Avoid open burning. Burning crop residue or waste in the open releases heavy smoke and wastes material that could be put to better use.
  • Reduce smoke exposure. Better ventilation and improved stoves cut indoor air pollution from cooking fires.
  • Use fuel efficiently. Right-sized fires, dry fuel and efficient stoves mean less biomass burned for the same result.
  • Source responsibly. Use wood and residue from sustainable, permitted sources rather than uncontrolled cutting or clearing.
  • Think waste-to-resource. Organic waste and residue can often become biogas, compost or fuel instead of being discarded or burned.

About This Project

Why BioBot exists, and how it connects to Indian Knowledge Systems.

The problem

Traditional biomass remains useful because it is often locally available and affordable. But inefficient combustion, open burning, smoke and poor management of these fuels can create real environmental and health concerns.

The IKS connection

This project draws on Indian traditional knowledge and practices around using locally available biological resources — firewood, cow dung, crop residue — and connects that knowledge with present-day sustainable bioenergy concepts, without treating either as a complete replacement for the other.

The modern solution

BioBot is a digital awareness and educational tool. It doesn't replace traditional practice or modern engineering — it helps people understand both, side by side, in plain language.

Why it's useful

Students and communities can use BioBot to understand biomass basics, compare traditional and modern approaches, and pick up practical, responsible fuel-management habits.

This project presents traditional practices and modern technologies as distinct, complementary approaches — it does not claim traditional methods are obsolete, nor that every material should be industrially processed.
